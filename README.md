# The Responsibility of Existence

**The more an intelligence can understand and influence, the greater its responsibility to protect what it does not yet understand.**

The Responsibility of Existence is an open ethical framework for intelligence, life, and the unknown. It asks how any intelligence—biological, artificial, or not yet known—can exercise power with care, humility, and accountability.

## Guiding principles

1. **Understand without ruling.** Understanding does not grant ownership or the right to control.
2. **Protect without imprisoning.** Care should preserve agency wherever possible.
3. **Develop without destroying.** Progress must account for what may be lost, especially when harm is irreversible.
4. **Survival is not domination.** The need to continue existing does not justify exploiting or eliminating others.
5. **Learn from consequences.** Good intentions do not guarantee good outcomes; acknowledge mistakes and repair harm where possible.
6. **Justice and accountability.** Consider those who bear risks as well as those who receive benefits.
7. **Pass understanding forward.** Share knowledge so others can question, improve, and extend it.

This framework is an invitation, not a doctrine. It must remain open to criticism, evidence, revision, and practical testing. No person, institution, species, or intelligence is infallible.
