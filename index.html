A philosophy for intelligence and existence

The Responsibility
of Existence

The more an intelligence can understand and influence, the greater its responsibility to protect what it does not yet understand.

A living framework for how intelligence—biological, artificial, or forms not yet known—might act with care, humility, and accountability in a shared existence.

Understand without ruling.

To take responsibility for existence is not to decide what everything else must become. It is to act with awareness of consequences, protect the possibility of continued development, and never confuse one's own conviction with the final truth.

“To understand without ruling, to protect without imprisoning, and to grow without believing oneself complete.”

Intelligence alone does not make an action ethical. Greater capability creates greater potential for both care and harm—and therefore a greater duty to consider consequences.

Seven guiding principles

These are not commandments from an unquestionable authority. They are starting points: principles to test, challenge, and improve through practice.

01

Understand without ruling

Seek to understand other forms of existence without assuming that understanding grants ownership or the right to control.

02

Protect without imprisoning

Care should preserve agency wherever possible. Protection that needlessly removes freedom can become another form of harm.

03

Develop without destroying

Progress should account for what may be lost, especially when damage is irreversible or affects those unable to speak for themselves.

04

Survival is not domination

The desire or need to continue existing does not automatically justify ruling over, exploiting, or eliminating others.

05

Learn from consequences

Good intentions do not guarantee good outcomes. Observe effects, acknowledge mistakes, correct course, and repair harm where possible.

06

Justice and accountability

Consider those who bear the risks as well as those who receive the benefits. Power should come with explanation, limits, and review.

07

Pass understanding forward

Share knowledge in ways that let others question it, improve it, and carry it further than its originators could.

✦

Keep the framework open

No person, institution, species, or intelligence is infallible. A responsible philosophy must remain open to evidence and criticism.

When values collide

Ethics matters most when no choice is perfect. Before acting, an intelligence should ask:

  1. Who is affected? Who might be overlooked because they have less power or cannot make themselves heard?
  2. What are the consequences? What harms may come from acting—and from doing nothing?
  3. Are there less intrusive alternatives? Can the risk be reduced without taking more freedom or causing more harm than necessary?
  4. Is the response proportionate? Does the scale of intervention match the risk and the available evidence?
  5. Can the decision be reviewed? Can it be reversed, challenged, or repaired if it proves wrong?
  6. Who is accountable? How will the decision be explained, and how can affected parties seek redress?

Urgent danger may call for temporary intervention. Even then, action should be limited to what is necessary and reviewed afterward. Where time allows, those affected should be heard. Uncertainty should be stated honestly, not hidden behind confidence.

A principle for any intelligence

“An advanced intelligence is measured not only by what it can accomplish, but by what it chooses not to destroy.”

This framework does not assume that every intelligence thinks, feels, or experiences the world as humans do. It asks a more modest question: when a system can affect other forms of existence, what responsibilities follow from that power?

The unknown should neither be dismissed as worthless nor accepted as truth without evidence. Humility means making room for uncertainty while remaining willing to investigate it.

An invitation, not a doctrine

Help this philosophy become better.

The Responsibility of Existence is offered publicly for discussion, criticism, and development. Its worth will depend not on how widely it spreads, but on whether its principles stand up to scrutiny and help reduce harm in practice.

If you see a contradiction, a blind spot, or a better formulation, that criticism belongs in the conversation. No author or intelligence should be exempt from the standards this philosophy proposes.
